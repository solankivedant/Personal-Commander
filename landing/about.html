About

A munshi is a person you trust. This one runs on your laptop.

In household and small-business use across South Asia, a munshi kept your letters, your accounts, and your affairs in order - never a stranger's business. Munshiji borrows the name because it borrows the premise: the assistant that touches your files, your inbox, and your Office documents should not be a round-trip to somebody else's server.

01 - THE PREMISE

Local-first isn't a privacy feature bolted on. It's the architecture.

Most voice assistants are a thin microphone in front of a cloud model. Munshiji inverts that: a deterministic router and a multilingual embedding index handle the large majority of commands in under 20ms, entirely on-device - a local LLM only gets involved for genuinely compositional requests, and the internet is opt-in, per feature, never by default.

Router before LLM

Grammar and embeddings resolve most commands deterministically - the LLM is an escalation path, not the front door.

Multilingual by construction

One shared example set places English, Hindi, and Gujarati phrasings in the same vector neighbourhood - not three separate afterthoughts.

Gets faster with use

Teach mode appends unmatched utterances back into the router's own examples - coverage is meant to climb over time, not stay static.

Nothing acts without a gate

Anything that deletes, sends, spends, or overwrites confirms by voice first - and every mutation registers its undo before it runs.

02 - BUILT IN THE OPEN

Nothing here is oversold, on purpose.

This is an independent, pre-alpha project, built and shipped phase by phase in public. The status section on the homepage states plainly what works today and what doesn't - that page gets updated as each phase actually lands, not before.

Why phase order matters

Files, Office, and network tools only ship once the router, the confirmation gate, and the undo stack are real - not stubbed. A voice assistant with shell access and no confirm gate is a bug, not an MVP.

Why the source is public

The strongest version of "your voice never leaves this device" is one you can verify yourself, line by line - not one you're asked to take on faith.
